# Persona

One player. Every feature in this app is built for them, and a decision that
does not serve them needs a different justification. See
[architecture.md](architecture.md) for the shape of the code that serves them.

## The player

**Sam, 31 — the plateaued hobby musician.**

Plays guitar and alto saxophone well enough to enjoy both. Learned by ear and by
tab, never by theory. Can hear that a groove is *doing something* — can even
play along by feel — but could not name the mode if asked, and knows that gap is
what keeps them repeating the same three shapes on the guitar and the same few
fingerings on the sax. The sax is in E♭, so a note name on the screen is not the
note they finger, and they have never quite worked out the offset.

Has a full-time job that isn't music. Practises in the gaps: twenty minutes
before dinner, an hour on a Sunday. Has started and abandoned three theory
courses, because a course is homework and they already have a job.

Plays Wordle every morning.

## What they want

- To get better at hearing, not at reading. Ear first, names second.
- A win in two minutes, on the phone, before the day starts.
- One thing per day. Not a curriculum, not a streak of guilt.
- To be told the answer eventually. Failing without learning is worse than
  losing.
- To pick up an instrument afterwards, if the groove is a good one — the guitar
  for chords and shapes, the sax for a line over the top.

## What loses them

- Being asked what they don't yet know: naked theory vocabulary, a wall of
  twelve roots and seven modes on day one.
- Homework. Anything that feels like a lesson plan, a syllabus, or a level.
- An account, a paywall, or a permission prompt before the first sound plays.
- A puzzle that is unwinnable, or one whose answer is never shown.
- Nothing new to come back to tomorrow.

## What this implies

- **The groove plays first.** The play button is the biggest thing on the page,
  and hitting it costs nothing — no sign-up, no tutorial to dismiss.
- **One puzzle a day, and it ends.** The session is short by design; the page
  stops at the puzzle rather than offering a library to get lost in.
- **Two difficulties, one game.** A simple mode for the ear that is still
  arriving, the full set of roots and modes for the ear that has.
- **Always resolvable.** Nudges narrow the options, and giving up reveals the
  answer. A day always closes with the player knowing more than they did.
- **The groove is worth playing along to.** It is the reward, not just the
  question — so it has to sound like music, at a stated tempo, in a loop you
  can jam over.
- **The lead register stays empty.** Sam brings the melody instrument, so the
  backing track is drums, bass and comp and nothing above the comp.
- **Two instruments, one answer.** What the reveal shows must be usable from
  both: a scale name and its notes serve the guitar as written, and the sax
  needs the same notes a major sixth up to finger them.
- **Progress lives in the browser.** No account is the feature, not a shortcut.

## Not the persona

Naming who we are *not* building for is what keeps the scope honest:

- **The trained musician** — a conservatory ear, who wants transcription
  drills and interval speed runs. They are welcome, and expert difficulty is
  for them, but the default page is not tuned to them.
- **The absolute beginner** — someone with no instrument and no ear yet. They
  need a course; this is a daily puzzle and it assumes you already play.
- **The theory student** — someone revising for an exam. They need coverage and
  a syllabus, which is exactly what one groove a day refuses to be.
